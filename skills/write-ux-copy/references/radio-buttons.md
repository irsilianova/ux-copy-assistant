# Radio buttons

## Definition

Radio buttons let someone choose one option from a group. Selecting a new option clears the previous selection. Use them when the options are mutually exclusive. If people can choose several answers together, use checkboxes.

## Core technique

Read the group heading as a question and each option as a possible answer. Try the same sentence frame with every option in the group. The bold words are the radio button labels.

| Intent | Sentence frame | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Choose a method | I’d like to pay by [option]. | I’d like to pay by **card** or **bank transfer**. |
| Set a frequency | Send me a summary [option]. | Send me a summary **once a day** or **once a week**. |
| Choose what happens next | I’d like to [option]. | I’d like to **pick it up** or **have it delivered**. |

Then check whether two options could describe the same answer. Under **How many people are coming?**, the options **1–2** and **2–4** both fit a party of two. **1–2**, **3–4**, and **5 or more** give each party size one clear answer.

## Language rules

### Allowed

- **A heading that names the choice.** Use a category (**Payment method**), a short instruction (**Choose a payment method**), or a question (**How would you like to pay?**). If the section heading already introduces the choice, use it as the group label.
- **Parallel options.** Use nouns, phrases, or short sentences. Keep the grammatical pattern consistent within a group. Under **Where should we send your receipt?**, **By email** and **By text** are easier to compare than **Email** and **Text it to me**.
- **Sentence case and minimal punctuation.** End a question heading with a question mark. Leave end punctuation off other group headings and individual options. If everyone needs the same explanation, put it below the heading as a complete sentence.

### Avoid

- **Redundant selection instructions.** Skip **Choose only one**. Use the heading to tell people what they’re choosing, as in **Which day works best?**
- **Choices that answer different questions.** Under **How would you like to pay?**, **By card** describes a method and **Pay later** describes timing. If someone can pay by card later, ask about method and timing separately.
- **Negative wording that takes work to decode.** Under **How often would you like a reminder?**, **Once a week** is clearer than **Don’t remind me every day**. The positive label tells someone which schedule they’re choosing.

### Use with caution

- **Long labels.** Aim for one or two words when they identify the choice clearly. Keep details that help people tell options apart, such as **This Friday** and **Next Friday**. If a label needs a second line, let it wrap so the full option stays visible.
- **Catch-all options.** Match the label to the answer it represents. Under **Which day works best?**, **Any day** means the person is flexible, **Not sure yet** means they haven’t decided, and **None of these days** means the proposed dates don’t work. Include the answers the flow can actually handle.
