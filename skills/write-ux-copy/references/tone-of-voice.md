# Tone of voice

## 1. General principle and character

The voice is clear, calm, and conversational. Sound like someone who understands the product and can explain it in everyday language. Help people answer the question they have at this moment: *What is this? What happened? What can I do next?*

Keep the voice consistent. Adjust how much warmth, detail, and direction you use according to the situation.

| Situation | How the tone changes | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routine task | Be brief and direct. Familiar actions need little explanation. | **Rename playlist** |
| First use | Be welcoming and concrete. Explain how to get started. | **No saved places yet.** Tap the bookmark on a place you’d like to visit. |
| Routine success | Confirm the outcome. A few words are usually enough. | **Changes saved** |
| Personal achievement | A little warmth fits when the person has accomplished something meaningful to them. | **You finished your first lesson!** |
| Error or interruption | Name the problem and give a practical next step. | **This file is too large. Choose one under 10 MB.** |
| Waiting or uncertainty | Say what the product can confirm and what happens next. | **We’ve received your application.** We’ll email you when there’s a decision. |
| Sensitive or consequential action | Use precise, neutral language. Give the person room to decide. | **Cancel this booking?** You’ll receive a €40 refund. **Cancel booking** / **Keep booking** |

## 2. Style

- **Use natural, conversational English.** Contractions such as **you’re**, **we’ll**, and **couldn’t** work well in sentences. Use the wording someone would recognize in context: **We couldn’t save your changes.**
- **Match the level of detail to the task.** After someone copies a link, **Link copied** is enough. After they cancel a subscription, **Your subscription is canceled. You can keep using it until 30 June.** answers the likely follow-up question.
- **Use warmth where it serves a purpose.** Acknowledgment can be as simple as **Thanks for the feedback.** For routine actions, skip greetings, praise, and commentary. Avoid jokes in errors, payments, cancellations, and other sensitive moments.
- **Keep punctuation understated.** Use sentence case. Let the wording carry the tone. An exclamation mark can fit a milestone, but routine confirmations like **Email updated** don’t need one.

## 3. Vocabulary

- **Choose familiar, specific words.** Prefer **use** to **utilize**, **end** to **terminate**, and **Try again** to **Reinitiate the process**. Keep a technical term when it helps someone recognize the right thing, as in **Upload a CSV file**.
- **Use one term for one concept.** If the product calls it a **workspace**, use **Create workspace**, **Workspace settings**, and **Leave workspace** throughout the flow. Switching to *team* halfway through can make people wonder whether they’re leaving something else.
- **Choose verbs that match the actual outcome.** **Request received**, **Refund approved**, and **Refund issued** describe different events. Use the one the product can verify.
- **Avoid words that judge the person’s effort or feelings.** Words like *just*, *simply*, *obviously*, and *easy* rarely help with instructions. **Enter the code from your email** gives the person everything they need.

## 4. Syntax

- **Lead with the information the reader needs first.** For an outcome, start with what happened: **Your booking is confirmed.** For a correction, start with what to do: **Choose an end date after the start date.**
- **Use active voice when the actor matters.** **We’ll email you when your order is ready** makes responsibility clear. A short result such as **Draft saved** works when the outcome matters more than the actor.
- **Give each sentence one main idea.** Separate the result from the next step when both matter: **Your email address has changed. Use the new address next time you sign in.**
- **Use fragments for labels and full sentences for explanations.** A heading can say **Delivery address**. Supporting text can say **We’ll send your replacement card to this address.**
- **Ask direct questions when the person has a decision to make.** **Discard your edits?** tells them what they’re deciding. Pair it with answers that name the outcomes: **Discard edits** / **Keep editing**.

## 5. Brand persona

The brand sounds like a **knowledgeable, unassuming guide**. It knows how the product works, takes responsibility for what the service does, and recognizes where its knowledge ends.

- **Knowledgeable.** Explain the detail that helps someone act. For a sharing setting, **Anyone with the link can view this file** is more useful than a broad description of access permissions.
- **Accountable.** Use **we** when the service owns the action or the problem: **We couldn’t send your invitation.** When someone needs to act, make that clear too: **Check the email address and try again.**
- **Honest about uncertainty.** If the product can verify submission but has no decision yet, say **We’ve received your request.** Promise a response time only when the service can meet it.

## 6. Relationship with the reader

Treat the reader as a capable person making their own decisions. They may be new to the product, short on time, or dealing with something that matters to them. Give them enough information to choose and enough context to understand the result.

- **Address the person directly when it adds clarity.** **Your receipt is ready** feels natural. In a form, **Email address** is usually enough.
- **Help people recover without assigning blame.** **Enter all 6 digits** gives a clear correction. **You entered the code incorrectly** makes the person responsible without showing them how to fix it.
- **Respect the choice to decline, leave, or stop.** Under **Get weekly updates?**, **Subscribe** and **No thanks** are both straightforward answers. Avoid refusal copy such as *No, I don’t want to stay informed*.
- **Avoid assuming an emotional reaction.** A refund, cancellation, or account closure can mean different things to different people. **Your account is closed** lets the reader interpret the outcome for themselves.
