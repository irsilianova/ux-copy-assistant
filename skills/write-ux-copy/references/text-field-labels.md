# Text field labels

## Definition

A text field label names the information someone needs to enter. It stays visible after they start typing so they can check what a value belongs to when reviewing the form.

## Core technique

Read the label as part of a sentence someone might say while filling in the field. The bold words are the label.

| Intent | Sentence frame | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enter a personal detail | My [label] is ___. | My **work email** is alex@example.com. |
| Identify the person the detail belongs to | The [label] is ___. | The **recipient’s name** is Sam Lee. |
| Name something | The [label] is ___. | The **playlist name** is Sunday mornings. |

Then read the label beside a filled-in value. Could someone tell what the value means when they come back to the form? **Name** beside **Sam Lee** could refer to the sender or the recipient. **Recipient’s name** resolves that ambiguity. If the section is already headed **Recipient details**, **Name** is enough.

## Language rules

### Allowed

- **Short noun phrases.** Name the information the field collects. **Email address**, **Order number**, and **Delivery notes** work as labels both before and after someone types. Aim for up to three words and use sentence case.
- **Qualifiers that change the answer.** Use **Work email** if a personal email would be the wrong one. Use **Email** if either address works.
- **Parallel wording for related fields.** If a form asks for two addresses, **Billing address** and **Shipping address** make the distinction easy to scan.

### Avoid

- **Instructions that repeat the act of typing.** In **Please enter your email address**, the words “please enter your” add little. **Email address** still makes sense when the field is filled in.
- **Examples as labels.** **City** tells someone what belongs in the field. **e.g., Belgrade** illustrates a possible answer and can go in the placeholder if an example helps. Give the field a label of its own.
- **Formatting rules inside the label.** Keep **Username** as the label and put **Use 3–20 letters or numbers** in helper text. Leave trailing colons off labels.

### Use with caution

- **Broad labels.** **Details** gives little guidance on a delivery form. **Delivery notes** tells someone that “Ring the upstairs bell” belongs there.
- **Required and optional markers.** Follow the form’s convention. If most fields are required, mark the exceptions, as in **Apartment number (optional)**. If the form uses an asterisk for required fields, explain it once and use it consistently.
