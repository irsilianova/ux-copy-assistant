# Buttons

## Definition

A button lets someone take an action, confirm a choice, or move through a task. Its label tells them what to expect when they tap it.

## Core technique

Read the label as something a person might say in context. Choose a frame that fits their intent. The bold words in each example are the button label.

| Intent | Sentence frame | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Take action | I want to [button label]. | I want to **buy tickets**. |
| Edit an item | I want to [button label] this [item]. | I want to **edit** this address. |
| Choose another way | I’d rather [button label]. | I’d rather **copy the link**. |
| Leave a step | I’d like to [button label]. | I’d like to **go back**. |

Then read just the heading and the labels together. Under **Ready to send?**, **Send invite** and **Keep editing** should let someone predict both outcomes without reading the body copy.

## Language rules

### Allowed

- **One action per button.** Start with a familiar verb and name the object when it helps. **Plan a trip** can open a planner. **Book trip** fits the step that makes the booking.
- **Short, natural labels.** Use sentence case and aim for up to three words. Keep the article when it clarifies a word’s part of speech. In **Open account**, “open” could be a verb or an adjective. **Open an account** makes the action clear. Allow for longer translations and wrapping when someone increases the text size.
- **Familiar labels for routine steps.** Use **Next** to advance through a sequence, **Try again** to repeat an attempt, and **Save changes** to finish editing. **Got it** or **OK** can dismiss an informational message.
- **Distinct choices.** For related actions, name what each verb applies to, as in **Send an email** and **Copy the link**. For opposite actions, make both outcomes explicit, as in **Cancel booking** and **Keep booking**.
- **A clear way out.** Use **Not now** to defer an offer, **No thanks** to decline it for good, **Skip** to bypass an optional step, and **Go back** to return to the previous screen.

### Avoid

- **Ambiguous answers.** Under **Delete this draft?**, use **Delete draft** and **Keep draft**. A bare **Yes** or **No** makes people look back at the question. A bare **Cancel** is especially confusing when cancellation is itself an option.
- **Extra words and visual clutter.** Drop “now” when timing is already clear. Leave out symbols, variable values, and punctuation such as commas, periods, and exclamation marks. **Add a guest** works even as the guest count changes.
- **Unfamiliar terms or explanations inside the button.** Use the words people have already seen on the screen. Put details that explain the action in the heading or body copy.

### Use with caution

- **Verbs without an object.** **Edit** works beside a saved address. **Confirm** works under the heading **Switch to weekly billing?** If the context leaves room for doubt, name the action more precisely.
- **First-person wording.** Most labels work without a pronoun. **I agree** is appropriate when tapping records the person’s explicit consent to the terms on screen.
